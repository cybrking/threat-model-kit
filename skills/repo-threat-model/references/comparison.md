# Comparison and synthesis

Research date: 2026-10-01. Descriptions follow linked primary sources. Advantages, disadvantages, and design choices are our practical assessment, not measured rankings. A threat taxonomy, a modeling process, an attack catalog, and a risk scoring method solve different problems and should not be treated as interchangeable frameworks.

| Method / technique | Primary purpose and advantage | Limitation / cost | What our framework keeps |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Four Question Framework / Manifesto](https://www.threatmodelingmanifesto.org/) | Simple lifecycle: understand the system, find threats, respond, review | Leaves elicitation, evidence, and prioritization details to practitioners | Four questions as the common process backbone |
| [STRIDE](https://learn.microsoft.com/azure/security/develop/threat-modeling-tool-threats), including per-element and per-interaction use | Repeatable security prompts: spoofing, tampering, repudiation, disclosure, denial of service, elevation of privilege | Categories alone do not establish attack feasibility, business importance, or privacy coverage; mechanical application creates noise | Baseline prompts at components and trust boundary crossings |
| [PASTA](https://versprite.com/cybersecurity-listings/offensive-security-services/application-threat-modeling/) | Seven-stage process connects business objectives, technical decomposition, threats, weaknesses, attacks, and risk | Full execution needs substantial context, expertise, and time; a rigid implementation can become burdensome | Harm-first scope, attack feasibility, business impact, proportionate analysis depth |
| [LINDDUN GO / PRO / MAESTRO](https://linddun.org/methods/) | Explicit privacy analysis; variants trade ease of use for systematic depth | Adds a separate analysis lens and expertise requirements; does not replace broader security analysis | Mandatory privacy applicability check, deeper analysis when personal data or inference is involved |
| [Attack trees](https://www.schneier.com/academic/archives/1999/12/attack_trees.html) and attack graph representations | Break goals into alternative paths and necessary conditions; graphs can represent shared dependencies | Complex models grow quickly; path completeness and numeric probabilities require justification | Explicit prerequisites and ordered paths in the core; AND/OR trees or graphs as optional extensions |
| [OCTAVE / Allegro](https://sei.cmu.edu/library/introducing-octave-allegro-improving-the-information-security-risk-assessment-process/) | Connects information assets to people, technology, facilities, and business processes; Allegro reduces assessment overhead | Organizational risk scope does not by itself supply implementation-level attack paths | Asset owners, consequences, operational dependencies, and risk acceptance |
| [Trike](https://trike.sourceforge.net/papers/Trike_v1_Methodology_Document-draft.pdf) | Models actors, assets, permitted actions, and rules before analyzing threats | Detailed permission models take effort and can miss harms outside the modeled rules | Explicit actor–action–resource invariants, checked against implementation |
| [VAST](https://www.threatmodeler.ai/innovation-lab/vast) | Emphasizes repeatability, automation, scale, and development integration; application and operational views help cover deployment context | Common implementation is vendor-associated; automated coverage depends on correct inputs and threat libraries | Incremental updates, reusable records, application plus deployment views; portable data contract |
| [Persona non Grata](https://sei.cmu.edu/library/a-hybrid-threat-modeling-method/) | Attacker motivations and capabilities make misuse concrete | Invented personas can bias analysis or exclude unexpected attackers | Capability-based actors, including insiders and compromised dependencies |
| [Security Cards](https://sei.cmu.edu/library/a-hybrid-threat-modeling-method/) | Creative prompts help discover unusual harms and attacks beyond a checklist | Brainstorming alone does not prioritize risks or demonstrate feasibility | A bounded discovery pass followed by evidence-based triage |
| [SEI Hybrid Threat Modeling Method](https://sei.cmu.edu/library/a-hybrid-threat-modeling-method/) | Combines structured preparation, creative elicitation, attacker thinking, and risk assessment | Combining methods alone does not demonstrate superior outcomes; pruning can discard uncertain but important risks | Multiple lenses, scenario normalization, retained uncertainty, explicit evaluation |
| [Quantitative TMM](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/blog/threat-modeling-12-available-methods/) | Combines STRIDE, component attack trees, and vulnerability scoring for interdependent systems | Vulnerability severity does not equal business risk; numeric outputs can imply unjustified precision | Dependency analysis; quantified estimates only with documented inputs |
| [SQUARE](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/library/security-quality-requirements-engineering-square/) | Agreed terminology and elicited, prioritized, inspected security requirements | Requirements engineering is broader than attack discovery and adds process effort | Clear invariants and testable mitigation requirements |
| [NIST SP 800-154, initial public draft](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/154/ipd) | Data-centric view helps track sensitive information across storage, processing, and transport | Selected-data focus can leave other harms uncovered; the linked publication is a draft | Data lifecycle and exposure analysis alongside system and actor views |
| [CSA MAESTRO for agentic AI](https://cloudsecurityalliance.org/blog/2025/02/06/agentic-ai-threat-modeling-framework-maestro) | Layered analysis covers AI agents, environments, interactions, and cross-layer threats | Emerging practice; layer classification does not prove controls effective and may duplicate baseline threats | Optional AI system profile covering models, data, tools, memory, orchestration, permissions, and deployment |

LINDDUN MAESTRO and CSA's agentic AI MAESTRO are different methods with the same short name. Using AI to assess a system does not imply the assessed system itself contains AI.

Adjacent techniques are useful inputs, not complete substitutes:

| Technique | Advantage | Disadvantage / guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| [MITRE ATT&CK](https://attack.mitre.org/resources/) / [CAPEC](https://capec.mitre.org/index.html) | Observed adversary behavior and reusable attack patterns make scenarios concrete | Catalog matches are leads, not proof of local applicability or exhaustive coverage |
| [DREAD](https://shostack.org/files/papers/modsec08/Shostack-ModSec08-Experiences-Threat-Modeling-At-Microsoft.pdf) | Accessible dimensions for discussing severity | Subjective arithmetic can create inconsistent rankings; retain explicit reasoning rather than a default composite score |
| [CVSS](https://www.first.org/cvss/) | Standardized vulnerability severity language | Does not measure whole-system business risk or threat model completeness; use only for applicable vulnerabilities |
| [FAIR](https://www.fairinstitute.org/hubfs/Standards%20Artifacts/Factor%20Analysis%20of%20Information%20Risk%20%28FAIR%29%20Standard%20v3.0%20%28January%202025%29.pdf) | Explicit loss-frequency and loss-magnitude reasoning supports investment decisions | Needs defensible estimates and uncertainty ranges; optional quantitative extension, not compulsory fabricated numbers |
| Abuse / misuse cases | Shows how legitimate business workflows can be manipulated | Depends on which workflows and harms are considered; use alongside component analysis |
| Kill-chain views | Helps reason about attack progression and detection opportunities | A fixed sequence can miss shortcuts, insider actions, or non-linear paths; do not force every scenario into it |

Abuse cases and kill chains appear in [OWASP's methods catalog](https://owasp.org/projects/threat-modelling-guide?tab=methods). OWASP's [Threat Modeling Project](https://owasp.github.io/www-project-threat-modeling/) explicitly supports multiple approaches rather than one official methodology.

The feasible objective is to reduce drawbacks with constraints: bounded analysis limits noise; explicit evidence limits invented certainty; profiles limit unnecessary process; versioned records limit staleness; shared IDs limit duplication. These introduce costs of their own: schema maintenance, evidence collection, and expert review. No framework can guarantee complete threat discovery or eliminate every tradeoff. SEI's [evaluation of methods](https://www.sei.cmu.edu/blog/evaluating-threat-modeling-methods-for-cyber-physical-systems/) also supports tailoring a combination rather than assuming one method covers everything.

Domain-specific work needs additional methods and qualified review, including safety/security interaction analysis, automotive TARA, or specialized privacy assessments. These are outside this general software framework's detailed coverage.
